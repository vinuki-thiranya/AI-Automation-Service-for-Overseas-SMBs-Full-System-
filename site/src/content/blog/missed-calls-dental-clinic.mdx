Every clinic misses calls. The phone rings while your receptionist is checking someone in, while the practice manager is on another line, at 7:55 am before anyone's at the desk, and all evening after you close. The question isn't how to never miss a call. It's what happens to the person on the other end when you do.

Many callers who reach voicemail don't leave a message. They hang up and try the next clinic on the list. If they're a new patient looking for a check-up, that's the end of it: you'll never know they called.

Here's how to catch more of those people, starting with the free fixes.

## 1. Find out how many calls you're missing

You can't fix what you can't see. Most phone systems, including the cheaper VoIP ones, show missed and abandoned calls. Pull a week of it and look at three things:

- **When** the missed calls happen. Usually it's the first hour of the day, lunch, and the last hour before close.
- **How many came back.** If a number appears once and never again, that's probably someone who went elsewhere.
- **How many were new numbers.** Existing patients call back. New ones often don't.

Even a rough count changes the conversation. "We miss a few calls" becomes "we missed 23 calls from new numbers last week, mostly between 8 and 9".

## 2. Fix the obvious gaps in the roster

If most misses happen in one window, it's often cheaper to stagger a shift than to add technology. Having someone start at 7:45 instead of 8:00, or covering lunch with a rolling break, catches the peak.

This helps during opening hours. It doesn't help at 8 pm.

## 3. Make your voicemail useful

If people do reach voicemail, give them a reason to do something other than hang up:

- Say when you'll call back ("before 10 am tomorrow").
- Give them another way to reach you: your online booking page or a number they can text.
- For a dental clinic, say what to do in an emergency, and to call 000 if it's life-threatening.

Keep it under 20 seconds. Nobody waits through a long greeting.

## 4. Text back every missed call

This is the single most effective change. When a call goes unanswered, the caller gets a text within seconds:

> Hi, sorry we missed your call to Harbourside Dental. How can we help? You can book here by text, or we'll call you back first thing.

It works because the caller is still holding their phone and still thinking about you. A text a few seconds later reaches them before they've dialled the next clinic. Often they want something simple: a time, a price, a yes or no about Saturdays. Many will happily sort it out by text.

You can do this manually, with someone working through the missed-call list. That works if you're disciplined and the list is short, but it rarely happens in the evening. Or you can automate it. Your phone provider forwards unanswered calls to a number that sends the text automatically.

## 5. Let them book in the same conversation

A text that says "call us back tomorrow" is better than nothing. A text that can actually book them in is much better.

This is where an assistant connected to your calendar earns its keep. The caller replies "do you have anything Thursday?", and it checks your real availability, offers two times, and books the one they choose. It does this at 9 pm as easily as at 9 am. Anything it can't handle, such as a complaint, a complicated treatment question or an emergency, goes to your team with a summary, so nobody has to scroll back through the thread.
