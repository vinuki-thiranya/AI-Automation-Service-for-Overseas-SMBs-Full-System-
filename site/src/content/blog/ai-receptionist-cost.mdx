"How much does an AI receptionist cost?" is a reasonable question with an annoying answer: it depends on what's included. Quotes range from a cheap monthly chatbot subscription to an expensive custom build. They're hard to compare, because they don't cover the same things.

Here's every cost involved, line by line, using our own prices as the worked example. Whoever you buy from, these are the lines to ask about.

## The three parts of the cost

An AI front desk has three kinds of cost:

1. **Setup**: connecting your channels and calendar, loading your services, prices and policies, and testing it.
2. **Running it**: hosting, monitoring, fixing things when providers change, and making changes as your clinic changes.
3. **Usage**: what the underlying services charge per message and per conversation.

Cheap subscriptions often bundle all three and leave out setup. You get a login and do the configuration yourself. Custom builds often quote setup and leave running costs vague. Ask for all three in writing.

## Setup: a fixed price

Our AI Receptionist is **USD 2,500**, once. That covers:

- WhatsApp, SMS and website chat, on your existing numbers.
- Booking, rescheduling and cancelling directly in your calendar or practice system.
- Missed-call text-back.
- Hand-off to your staff with a summary when the assistant can't help.
- After-hours wording and emergency directions.
- Testing against 50 conversations, including the awkward ones: someone trying to get a discount, someone describing symptoms, someone asking for a price that isn't on your list.
- A recorded walkthrough, staff training and a one-page guide.

Most clinics add lead follow-up for ads and web forms. The two together are **USD 4,000**. A voice agent that answers the phone itself is an extra USD 1,000.

Ask any vendor: *is the price fixed, and what exactly is in it?* If the answer is an hourly estimate, expect it to grow.

## Running it: the care plan

Software that talks to your patients needs looking after. WhatsApp changes its rules, calendar APIs change, you add a new service or a new dentist. Our care plans start at **USD 300 a month**. That covers hosting, monitoring, the API keys and two hours of changes. **USD 600** adds six hours and a monthly review. The minimum is three months, then it runs month to month.

Ask: *who fixes it when it breaks, and how quickly?* "Email us" with no response time is a warning sign.

## Usage: passed through, capped

Every message costs something: the AI model that reads and replies, the SMS, the WhatsApp conversation. For a typical clinic it's small, but it's real, and it grows with volume.

We pass usage through at cost plus 20% and agree a monthly cap up front, usually between USD 50 and 150. You can see the providers' invoices any time.

Ask: *how is usage charged, is there a cap, and can I see the underlying bills?* A flat "unlimited" price usually means the vendor has a cap they haven't told you about, or a margin they'd rather you didn't see.

## Is it worth it?

Do the sum for your own clinic: what is a new patient worth to you over their first year? Then ask how many extra bookings a month would cover the monthly cost. For most clinics the answer is a handful. That's why the honest test is to look at your missed calls and after-hours messages before buying anything.
