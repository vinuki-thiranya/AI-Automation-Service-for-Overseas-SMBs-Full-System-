Your clinic closes at six. Your patients don't stop needing you at six. They message after dinner, on Sunday afternoons, and late at night when a tooth starts throbbing. What they read in reply sets the tone for whether they book with you or keep looking.

A good after-hours message does four jobs:

1. **Says you're closed, and when you'll be back.** Be specific: "tomorrow from 8 am", not "during business hours".
2. **Tells them what they can do right now.** Book online, leave their details, or keep chatting if an assistant can book them in.
3. **Handles emergencies safely.** Where to go tonight, and 000 if it's life-threatening.
4. **Sounds like your clinic.** Warm and brief, the way your front desk would say it.

Here are templates for each channel. Copy them, then change the details.

## SMS or WhatsApp auto-reply

Keep it short. People read texts on their phones, often in one glance.

> Thanks for messaging Harbourside Dental. We're closed now and back tomorrow from 8 am. To book, reply with a day that suits, or use example.com/book. For a dental emergency tonight, call [your local emergency dental service], or 000 if it's life-threatening.

If an assistant can book after hours, say so. It changes what people do next:

> Thanks for messaging Harbourside Dental. We're closed, but I'm the clinic's virtual assistant and I can still book you in or answer questions. What can I help with?

## Missed-call text-back

When a call rings out after hours, a text that arrives seconds later stops people from dialling the next clinic:

> Hi, sorry we missed your call to Harbourside Dental. We're closed until 8 am, but you can book by replying here, or we'll call you back first thing. In an emergency tonight, call 000.

## Voicemail

Under 20 seconds. Lead with the most useful thing:

> You've reached Harbourside Dental. We're closed and back at 8 am. The fastest way to book is to text this number, and you'll get an answer straight away. If you have a dental emergency, call [your local emergency dental service], or 000 if it's life-threatening.

## Website chat

People on your website are already looking at you. Don't send them away:

> We're closed right now, but I can book you in, tell you about prices and health funds, or help with directions and parking. What are you after?

## Getting the emergency wording right

Write this part with your dentists, not your marketing person. It should:

- name a real, local after-hours option and check that it still exists every few months;
- say plainly to call 000 for anything life-threatening, such as trouble breathing or swallowing, or swelling that's spreading fast;
- never try to diagnose or advise on treatment by message.

If you use an assistant, make sure it gives the same emergency wording every time and doesn't improvise. Ours are set up to give your exact text and offer the first emergency slot in the morning, without commenting on symptoms.

## A note on tone

After-hours messages are often written defensively ("Our office is currently closed. Please call during business hours."). It's accurate and unhelpful. Imagine someone who's been putting off a check-up for a year, finally messaging at 9:47 pm. One friendly line and a way to book is often all it takes.
